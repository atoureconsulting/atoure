# Company Overview

## What AToure is

AToure Management & Consulting is a management and consulting company, not an agency. The distinction matters and should be kept in every piece of writing about the company, in English and in French: "agence" is out in French copy in the same way "agency" is out in English. The one place the word survives is the contractual term "agency fee" used in the Mauritius documents, which is a fee name rather than a description of what AToure is.

The difference between an agency and a management and consulting company, as AToure frames it, is execution. An agency sells a campaign and hands delivery to someone else. AToure builds the project and runs it on the ground itself, as the operator: sourcing the creator or talent, negotiating the commercial deal, securing the visas and permits, mobilising security and transport, and staying present through delivery. Talent discovery, brand matchmaking and operational execution sit inside one team, working to one standard, rather than being split across a chain of vendors and handoffs.

## Leadership

Baba Touré is Director of AToure, not founder. His own account of starting the company centres on a gap he saw in how the industry treated creators: too many agencies dealt with creators as numbers in a database, focused on reach and KPIs rather than relationships. AToure was built to close that gap by stepping into the middle of the relationship between brands, creators and markets, and making it personal.

Touré's own network and work run through trade, investment and everyday business as well as social media, opening a route for global brands to build in Africa and for African companies and creators to reach the world. That network spans West Africa, North Africa, the UK and the Middle East, including partnerships with government entities: ministries, tourism boards, security services and municipal authorities as well as multinationals and grassroots creators.

## How AToure operates

AToure's core claim is that it closes the execution gap between a good idea and a delivered campaign. In practice this means:

- A single creator database and matchmaking function, so brand and creator fit is based on audience geography, cultural alignment and content fit, not just follower counts.
- In market operational capability: visas, permits, local production and crew, security, transport, hospitality and vendor payment, handled by people who already work in that market rather than assembled for a single trip.
- Institutional access already in place before a brief lands: government and ministerial relationships, tourism boards, security services and local sponsors that AToure has already built trust with, rather than starting from zero once a client signs.
- Cultural fluency treated as a foundation rather than a feature: campaigns are framed for African audiences from the start, not adapted afterward.

AToure describes itself as having a ground level presence across 17 countries, spanning West Africa, the Middle East, Europe and beyond, and states 12 or more active markets across Africa, Europe and the Middle East, three continents run from one desk. A separate internal list of countries the company has activated in names the United Kingdom, France, Spain, Qatar, Morocco, the United Arab Emirates, Ivory Coast, Germany, the Republic of Congo, Turkey and Monaco; that list should be treated as a working record rather than the company's full footprint, since the two figures come from different sources and have not been reconciled against each other.

AToure states that its campaigns have generated more than 7 billion views in total, and is based in London, where it was established in 2023. Proposal documents for Ivorian mandates give AToure's operating base as London and Abidjan.

## Core value proposition

The pitch AToure makes to brands and creators is access plus execution: "We're already in the markets you're trying to reach." Most agencies start building relationships after a client signs; AToure's argument is that it has already spent years earning trust across African and diaspora markets, so once a brief lands, it can move immediately rather than needing a lead in time to build the infrastructure a campaign depends on.
