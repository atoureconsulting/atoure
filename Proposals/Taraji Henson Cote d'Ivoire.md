# Taraji P Henson, Cote d'Ivoire

Confidential proposal, prepared for Rolling Stone Africa, August 2026. This is a pitch, not a delivered mandate.

## The concept

Three themes run through the proposed journey: Ivorian cinema, the shea butter industry, and the role of women across both. Taraji P Henson is positioned as an actress and a founder in the women's beauty industry, meeting a country with a cinema culture that reaches the world and a shea butter sector built almost entirely by women's hands. The proposal is explicit that Rolling Stone Africa owns the documentary and its editorial direction, and that the film should read as a Rolling Stone Africa production about Cote d'Ivoire, never as a brand film.

## Cinema chapter

The proposal names several figures in Ivorian film to show the depth of the local community. None had been approached and none was a confirmed participant at the time of writing; the final cast was to be agreed with the Rolling Stone Africa production team. Names cited: Akissi Delta (actress turned filmmaker, creator of the series Ma Famille), Naky Sy Savané (best actress awards at Vues d'Afrique and at Khouribga in 1994, and a campaigner for women's and children's rights), Philippe Lacôte (director of Night of the Kings, which reached the Academy Award shortlist for International Feature), Michel Gohou (actor, from Les Guignols d'Abidjan), Diêm Camille (actress and director, whose short film Doll won five awards), and Fat Touré (in 2025, the first francophone actress nominated for best actress at the Africa International Film Festival).

## Shea butter chapter

Framed around value and ownership rather than scarcity: shea butter is collected, sorted, roasted, ground, kneaded and extracted almost entirely by women in the northern savannah around Korhogo, and gains most of its value once it leaves the country. The proposal notes that 9 in 10 shea butter producers in Cote d'Ivoire are women, and that shea butter sits among the country's top five agricultural exports. These sector figures are explicitly flagged in the source document itself as indicative, to be confirmed with the relevant Ivorian institutions before publication, and no specific cooperative had yet been approached.

## Personal grounding for the pitch

The proposal builds its case for Henson from her own public statements rather than only her filmography: her account of building TPH by Taraji to protect her scalp under protective styles, buying the brand back outright in April 2025, and a December 2023 SiriusXM interview in which she described being paid a fraction of the value she created on a past project and having to renegotiate from the bottom each time. AToure's own framing is that these details were found in her own words, not assumed.

## Proposed itinerary shape

Seven days. Day 1 is arrival only. Days 2 to 5 are the working content days, with the cover shot in Korhogo on day 5. Days 6 and 7 return to Abidjan and close the story. The document is explicit that this is a starting point for a working session with the Rolling Stone Africa production team, not a finished schedule.

## Why AToure, per the proposal

Same core claims as the Janet Jackson proposal: direct institutional access already activated on delivered projects, security at national scale (citing more than 600 personnel mobilised for IShowSpeed), sponsors closed in market from zero (MTN, Solibra, Snedai), and the 1.57 billion view Ashton Hall Africa Tour plus the 6.1 billion digital impression AFCON Morocco figure cited as proof (impressions should be treated as modelled per house style). Institutional access named additionally includes the Ministry of Women, Family and Children, given the shea cooperative focus, and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Agriculture, given the export and value addition angle.

## Commercial structure proposed

Same two stream structure as the Janet Jackson proposal: a fixed organisation fee for AToure as ground operating partner, and a success commission on sponsorship AToure sources.
