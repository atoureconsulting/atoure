# Ashton Hall Africa Tour

Delivered 2026. AToure's largest single creator mandate to date: five African countries, built and run country by country. Described on the site as a featured mandate.

## Countries and order

Ghana (Accra), Cameroon (Douala), Nigeria (Lagos), Benin (Cotonou), Rwanda (Kigali), in that order. Douala was the only Cameroonian market on the tour.

## The mission

The stated goal was to turn international attention toward Africa through a creator of Ashton Hall's calibre, and to build a genuine bond with the people on the ground in each country. Every leg protected his artistic direction while making room for real impact: ministerial and top level government meetings, sessions with Don Jazzy and Dr Sid, football with local kids, and partnerships with foundations doing lasting work.

## What AToure delivered

- Partnerships, collaborations and sponsors
- Media coverage
- Visas, security and transport
- Itinerary and experience curation in every country

## Leg by leg

### Leg 1, Ghana, Accra
Opening leg. Hard training in the morning, culture and crowds by the afternoon. Featured post: the Ashton Hall x Ashton Small collab in the streets of Accra, 26.1 million views, 1.9 million likes, 16.6 thousand comments, 29.2 thousand shares, 224 thousand sends. The @ashton_small01 version of the same collab reached 50.1 million views.

### Leg 2, Cameroon, Douala
Described as the rawest leg: street training with local athletes and crowds that filled entire blocks. Featured post, "The Market Lift," a working morning in a Douala market: 44.3 million views across two feed posts. The @ashtonhall version reached 32.5 million views and 1.4 million likes; the @ashton_small01 version, "a work day in Cameroon," reached 11.8 million views and 506 thousand likes.

### Leg 3, Nigeria, Lagos
The commercial centre of the tour: industry meetings by day, the single largest crowd of the trip. Featured post, "The Stadium Run," a morning run through a Lagos stadium that became a crowd event: 13 million views, 548 thousand likes, 8,492 comments, 11.4 thousand shares, 10.1 thousand sends. @indian_ashtonhall joined the tour on this leg.

### Leg 4, Benin, Cotonou
The cultural heart of the tour and its closing ceremony: Vodun custodians, markets and a send off through the city. Featured post, "The Sack Lift," raw strength filmed in a working yard in Cotonou: 31.9 million views, 1.4 million likes, 15.3 thousand comments, 16.4 thousand shares, 17.8 thousand sends. Benin returned the highest engagement per view of any leg on the tour.

### Leg 5, Rwanda, Kigali
The quietest and most reflective leg: less spectacle, more time with communities. Featured post, "Day 14," a face mask morning routine filmed in Kigali, became the single largest post of the entire Africa Tour at 171 million views, 7.5 million likes, 42.4 thousand comments, 119 thousand shares, 633 thousand sends. The leg that carried it produced three of the tour's top five performing posts, including an Ankole cattle post at 56.7 million views and a "God is amazing" night post at 35.1 million views.

## Press

National press picked up the tour in all five countries, in English, French and Kinyarwanda. Fourteen articles across five countries in three languages, with international syndication onto MSN. The Rwanda leg alone drew coverage from six outlets. Coverage was national and trade press only, and excludes the far larger volume of social and creator led coverage across Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and X in each market.

## Note on an earlier draft of this tour

An earlier internal proposal and an earlier working itinerary document, both prepared before the final tour, described a different route (variously including Morocco, Senegal, Ivory Coast and Ethiopia, timed around the 2026 World Cup) and a different, shorter routing through Ghana, Ivory Coast, Nigeria and Benin. Neither matches the tour that actually ran. Treat the country list above, Ghana, Cameroon, Nigeria, Benin, Rwanda, as the confirmed and delivered route, and treat the earlier drafts as superseded planning history rather than fact about what was delivered.
